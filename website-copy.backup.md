## Why I believe I can tango...

Don’t like talking too much about myself, so here’s the highlight reel…

- **Took the non-traditional route.** Worked directly with a proptech CEO at 17, did door-to-door sales, climbed on roofs and installed gutters, then landed a software-company internship at 19. Four senior engineering hires later, they offered me a full-time recruiting role.
- **Helped stand up Nubank’s first new-market talent hub, in Canada.** Built as we went, owned the early engineering funnel, and delivered 12 Canada hires by mid-April, plus a US placement from that same funnel.
- **Got pulled into the Palo Alto tiger team.** Our mandate: roll out a faster hiring strategy while keeping quality high — filling 180 lead-to-senior-staff+ roles (IC6–IC9+) to stand up six engineering teams across AI, ML, SWE and Crypto.
- **Maintained 65% pass-through at the first technical stage (TS1).** For candidates coming through my screens. Defined an evidence-based screen, helped set the technical recruiting standard, and coached the team to that bar.
- **Closed senior technical talent — and hired the people above me.** An IC8 close in my first eight months, plus our TA Manager and Global Mobility Senior Manager. One is now my manager (still a funny thing to say out loud).
- **Became the go-to in a 110-person TA org.** For outreach, creative sourcing, candidate experience, and the “can you look at this?” questions. I like being the person the team can hand the hard thing to.
- **Kept building tools because I can’t help myself.** A Claude screening agent teammates used in real recruiting, sourcing dashboards, and role-scoring criteria to turn hiring-manager expectations into something the team could use.
- **Made candidate experience part of the work.** Wrote engineering-reviewed interview prep guides that grew into nine docs across three languages. Candidates, including people we didn’t hire, said screens felt like “connecting with a friend.”

Still working on getting to the recommendation faster and measuring where my AI tools actually help — clearer reasoning, feedback from the team, and reviewing the misses.
